Day 3 – Probability

Today I revisited probability — but this time through the lens of data analysis, not just theory.

I’ve always known probability as “the chance of an event happening.”
But today it clicked differently.

Probability is the foundation of prediction.

If data analysis explains what happened,
probability helps us reason about what could happen.

That shift in perspective was important for me.

I also learned about probability distributions, which describe how outcomes are structured:
	•	Discrete distributions deal with outcomes you can count (like number of customers or number of clicks).
	•	Continuous distributions deal with outcomes you measure (like time, weight, revenue).

Then I explored common distributions and started seeing how they connect to real-world scenarios:
	•	Normal distribution – the classic bell curve. Many real-world variables cluster around an average.
	•	Uniform distribution – every outcome has equal likelihood.
	•	Poisson distribution – useful for modeling how often events occur in a fixed interval (like arrivals or system failures).
	•	Exponential distribution – often used to measure time between events.

What stood out to me today:

Different problems require different distributions.

You can’t model customer arrivals the same way you model exam scores.
Choosing the wrong distribution means misunderstanding the problem.

I’m starting to see that probability isn’t abstract math — it’s a decision-making tool.
